P  33 [Heading 1] Abstract / Executive Summary
P  34 [None] Liquidity providers (LPs) on Uniswap V3 must choose a concentrated price range for their capital at the moment a position is opened, a decision that is the single largest driver of whether the position ultimately earns a profit or a loss, yet is typically made with little systematic guidance. This project frames LP profitability as a binary classification problem: predict, using only information available at open time (chosen tick range, pool characteristics, deposit composition, pre-open market conditions, and wallet experience), whether a position will close profitably.
P  35 [None] A leakage-safe dataset of 62,250 closed Uniswap V3 positions was built from nine on-chain data sources (Dune Analytics and Google BigQuery), covering the ten highest-volume pools from Uniswap V3's launch through end of 2023. The pipeline resolved multi-table joins across position open, close, mint, and fee-collection events; corrected a token-decimals unit error and a corrupted-value outlier bug that would otherwise have silently distorted the profitability label; and explicitly excluded every feature only knowable after a position closes, including two columns (position duration and partial-withdrawal count) not caught in earlier data audits.
P  36 [None] A time-based 80/20 train/test split (no shuffling, to prevent temporal leakage) was used to train a baseline Logistic Regression and a challenger XGBoost classifier. Variable selection combined a filter pass (variance and correlation checks) with embedded methods (L1-regularized logistic regression and Random Forest importance), cross-checked against raw class frequencies to catch unstable coefficients driven by rare categories.
P  37 [None] The final XGBoost model achieved a test ROC-AUC of 0.596 and Gini of 0.191, a modest but genuine and non-leaked discriminative signal. A learning-curve analysis showed the gap between training and test performance is driven substantially by a real shift in market conditions between the 2021–2022 and 2023 portions of the study period (profitable-position rate rises from 58.3% in training to 67.0% in test), rather than classical overfitting alone. The headline finding is that open-time features carry real, measurable predictive signal for LP profitability, but the current feature set and single-regime training window likely limit accuracy; expanding the pool universe, extending the time range, and adding wallet-behavior features are identified as the highest-value next steps.

P  39 [Heading 1] Chapter 1: Introduction
P  40 [Heading 2] 1.1 Background & Motivation
P  41 [None] Uniswap V3 changed how automated market makers work by letting liquidity providers concentrate their capital within a chosen price range, rather than spreading it across the entire price curve as in V2. This concentration multiplies fee income per dollar deposited when the market price stays inside the chosen range, but exposes the position to impermanent loss and to earning zero fees if the price moves outside it. The range choice is therefore the single biggest lever an LP controls, and most LPs currently choose it by rule of thumb rather than by any systematic, data-driven process.
P  42 [None] This project is Nash's M.Sc Big Data Analytics final project and is also intended as a portfolio piece targeting on-chain data analyst roles, which shaped several practical decisions throughout (documenting real data-engineering constraints, for instance, rather than hiding them).
P  43 [Heading 2] 1.2 Business Problem Statement
P  44 [None] An LP opening a Uniswap V3 position today has no reliable, quantitative way to estimate whether their chosen range, pool, and deposit will be profitable, beyond intuition and after-the-fact observation of similar positions. This project addresses that gap by building a model that scores a proposed position's likely profitability using only information available at the moment of opening, before any capital is at risk of a bad range choice.
P  45 [Heading 2] 1.3 ML Problem Formulation
P  46 [None] Target variable: profitable, a binary label (1 = position closed with net economic profit, including fees earned, net of impermanent loss; 0 = otherwise).
P  47 [None] Unit of analysis: one closed Uniswap V3 LP position (one NFT position-manager tokenId).
P  48 [None] Moment of prediction: the block timestamp at which the position is opened (open_time). Every feature in the final model is constructed to be knowable strictly at or before this moment; label-construction fields that are only knowable at close (close price, fees eventually collected, position duration, count of partial withdrawals) are explicitly excluded from the feature matrix.
P  49 [Heading 2] 1.4 Objectives & Success Metrics
P  50 [List Paragraph] Business KPI: enable an LP to rank candidate range/pool choices by predicted profitability before committing capital.
P  51 [List Paragraph] Statistical metric: ROC-AUC and Gini on a chronologically held-out test set, reported alongside KS, PR-AUC, and a full decile/lift table per the course's evaluation standard.
P  52 [Heading 2] 1.5 Scope & Constraints
P  53 [None] Scope: the ten highest-volume Uniswap V3 pools by swap activity, covering positions opened between 2021-05-01 (V3 launch) and 2024-01-01. P  54 [None] A further scope note is documented explicitly in Chapter 10.6: PSI/CSI stability analysis is not applicable to this project as scoped, since there is no live production scoring population to monitor, only a static historical train/test split.

P 100 [Heading 1] Chapter 4: Data Cleaning
P 101 [Heading 2] 4.1 Missing Value Treatment
P 102 [None] Price-join misses: positions whose open or close minute had no exact USD price match were dropped rather than imputed, since fabricating a price for the profitability label itself would corrupt the target variable. This filter reduced the dataset from 154,740 candidate positions to 62,265 rows with full open- and close-price coverage (63,988 rows missing a close price, 61,183 missing an open price, with some overlap).
P 103 [None] A small number of pre-open volatility-window values (24h rolling price statistics) and momentum values were unavailable for positions too early in a token's price history to have a full trailing window; these were dropped (volatility window) or filled with a neutral 0 (momentum, treated as "no measurable momentum" rather than a fabricated value) as documented directly in the pipeline.
P 104 [Heading 2] 4.2 Outlier Treatment
P 105 [None] A genuine data-corruption issue, not organic outliers, was found and fixed: on-chain deposit amounts (mint_amount0/1) occasionally carried corrupted or overflowed values up to roughly 1e31 in magnitude, six or more orders beyond any realistic position size, which propagated to infinite values after downstream arithmetic. The outlier filter was originally scoped only to the withdrawal-side column (total_token0_returned_adj); describe() review during development revealed the deposit-side columns carried the same corruption and needed the identical filter. Rows with any of the three amount columns exceeding 1e10 (in human-readable, decimal-adjusted units) were dropped.
P 106 [Heading 2] 4.3 Inconsistent & Impossible Values
P 107 [None] Token amounts were converted from on-chain base units to human-readable units by dividing by 10^decimals per token (USDC/USDT: 6, WETH/DAI: 18, WBTC: 8). This is a unit-scalar correction, not a precision rounding step — getting it wrong shifts values by many orders of magnitude and can corrupt the sign of the profitability comparison itself, so it was applied before any profitability arithmetic.
P 108 [Heading 2] 4.4 Duplicate Handling
P 109 [None] Mint-to-open-event matching required resolving a many-to-many join risk: a subset of transactions contained multiple Mint events, multiple Open events, or both. Single-mint transactions were joined directly; multi-mint transactions were resolved by matching on liquidity_added amount, falling back to a top-up events table where the primary open-events table had no match. tokenIds whose Mint-derived tick range was inconsistent across resolution paths were dropped entirely rather than guessed at.
P 110 [None] Separately, an earlier version of the Close and Collect queries used a "take the last event per position" pattern that silently discarded amounts from any earlier partial withdrawal or fee collection — affecting roughly 8% of positions (13,592 of 171,707) in the raw extraction. This was fixed by switching to a GROUP BY tokenId with SUM() aggregation, which correctly captures total value across all partial events and is the version used in the final v5 dataset.

P 119 [Heading 2] 5.5 Session-2 Robustness
P 120 [None] A session-2 robustness study tested whether four cyclical open-time features and two interaction features could improve the V0 model. The time features are open_hour_sin, open_hour_cos, open_dow_sin and open_dow_cos, encoding the position's open hour and weekday (UTC) as sin/cos pairs so the two ends of the day and week are adjacent in feature space. All 24 hours and all 7 weekdays are present in both the training window (626 days) and the test window (343 days). The two interactions are algebraic products of train-fitted z-scores of range_width_normalized and token0_price_std_24h, and of fee_tier_pct and pre_open_avg_daily_swap_count_3d, following the domain hypothesis that range width should matter most when pre-open volatility is high, and that fee tier interacts with the pool's liquidity depth.
P 121 [None] Four variants were compared on the identical chronological split (V0 = the existing 27 features, V1 = V0 + time features, V2 = V0 + interactions, V3 = V0 + both). For each variant the Logistic Regression and the tuned XGBoost (fixed Block 3 hyperparameters, no re-tuning) were scored with 5-fold TimeSeriesSplit on TRAIN ONLY for selection, and the test-set battery was reported with its threshold re-derived from train-set macro-F1. The leakage guard (test ROC-AUC more than about 0.02 above the 0.5955 baseline) never tripped.

P 123 [None] Verdict: no variant improved train-CV ROC-AUC by more than the ~0.001 noise floor or the fold standard deviation; the largest positive delta is V1's XGBoost +0.0007 (CV 0.5943 versus 0.5936), an order of magnitude below its fold sd of 0.0146. P 124 [Heading 1] Chapter 6: Feature Selection
P 125 [Heading 2] 6.1 Method(s) Used
P 126 [None] With 62,250 rows and roughly 30 candidate features, this is not the high-dimensional (p ≫ n) regime the course's gene-expression case study targets, so the full filter→wrapper→embedded funnel was not necessary. A combined filter + embedded approach was used instead:
P 127 [List Paragraph] Filter: near-zero-variance check (none found) and pairwise correlation check at a 0.9 threshold (no pairs found above threshold).
P 128 [List Paragraph] Embedded: L1-regularized (Lasso) logistic regression on scaled features, and Random Forest impurity importance on unscaled features, as two independent multivariate signals.
P 129 [None] The two embedded methods disagreed sharply on two features: range_overlaps_recent_trading and a low-frequency pool dummy (pool_address for the 899-row, 1.8%-of-training pool) both received very large Lasso coefficients but near-zero Random Forest importance. Investigation traced this to class skew and sample size rather than a data error: range_overlaps_recent_trading is 99.0% one value in training data (only 514 of 49,800 rows are the minority class), and the flagged pool dummy is built from under 2% of training rows. Both patterns are known to produce large, unstable linear coefficients while contributing little to tree-based impurity reduction, which is well-documented in the course's stability-selection material. Since the deployed model is tree-based (XGBoost), Random Forest's judgement on these features was weighted more heavily; both features were retained (a tree ensemble handles rare categories and skewed binary flags more gracefully than a linear coefficient does), but Lasso's specific coefficient magnitudes for the low-frequency pool are flagged in this report as unreliable rather than treated as equally trustworthy evidence.
P 130 [None] A wrapper stage, recursive feature elimination with cross-validation (RFECV, step=1, 5-fold TimeSeriesSplit, scoring ROC-AUC), was added as a session-2 robustness check of the filter and embedded rankings. Logistic Regression selected 14 of 27 features (best CV ROC-AUC 0.5639, versus 0.5627 on the full set), and the tuned XGBoost selected 21 of 27 (CV 0.5962 versus 0.5936 on the full set; test 0.5982 on the subset versus 0.5955 on the full set). The tree model's RFECV ranking agrees with the embedded impurity ranking on the dominant features, and the selection gain (CV +0.003) is inside the fold standard deviation, so the full 27-feature V0 set remains the deployed feature list (Chapter 6.2 unchanged). The elimination curve is shown as Figure 13 in Appendix C.

P 134 [Heading 1] Chapter 7: Data Preprocessing & Train/Test Strategy
P 135 [Heading 2] 7.1 Train/Test Split Strategy
P 136 [None] An 80/20 split by open_time, with no shuffling, was used throughout. This is chronological on-chain data: a random split would let the model train on positions that opened after some test-set positions had already closed, implicitly leaking knowledge of market regimes that hadn't occurred yet at certain training points. The split point falls at 2023-01-21, giving 49,800 training rows (2021-05-05 to 2023-01-21) and 12,450 test rows (2023-01-21 to 2023-12-31).
P 137 [Heading 2] 7.2 Scaling & Encoding Pipeline
P 138 [None] StandardScaler was fit on training data only and applied to both splits; binary/one-hot columns were left unscaled. pool_address one-hot dummy columns were generated from the training set's categories and aligned onto the test set (any category absent from test padded with zeros) to guarantee identical column structure between splits.
P 139 [Heading 2] 7.3 Handling Class Imbalance
P 140 [None] Not applicable in the SMOTE/class-weighting sense: the target is well-balanced overall (60/40). The more relevant imbalance in this project is the train/test class-balance shift discussed in Chapter 2.4 and revisited as an overfitting confound in Chapter 10.5, which is a temporal distribution issue rather than a rare-event problem.
P 141 [Heading 2] 7.4 Cross-Validation Design
P 142 [None] 5-fold chronological cross-validation was run via scikit-learn's TimeSeriesSplit, which guarantees every fold trains on an earlier chronological block and validates on a later one, preserving the no-shuffle discipline used throughout this project (fold 1: train on the first 8,300 rows, validate on the next 8,300; fold 5: train on the first 41,500 rows, validate on the final 8,300). This was used both for the hyperparameter search in Chapter 9 and as the model-selection stability check the course requires.
P 143 [None] Per-fold ROC-AUC for the eventually-selected hyperparameters: 0.588, 0.586, 0.605, 0.579, 0.610 (mean 0.594, standard deviation 0.012). A standard deviation this small, well under the 0.05 threshold used as a stability red flag throughout this project, indicates the model's modest performance is consistent across different chronological windows of the training period — the weakness diagnosed in Chapter 10 is a genuine, stable property of the feature set, not an artifact of one lucky or unlucky split.

P 146 [None] Logistic Regression (C=1.0, features scaled) was fit as the interpretable baseline. Test performance: ROC-AUC 0.580, Gini 0.159, KS 11.8, PR-AUC 0.725, Brier 0.221. This is the bar the challenger model must clear.
P 147 [Heading 2] 8.2 Candidate Models
P 148 [List Paragraph] Logistic Regression — fast, transparent, coefficients directly readable; reasonable candidate given no legal/regulatory requirement here, but included primarily as the disciplined baseline the course methodology requires.
P 149 [List Paragraph] XGBoost — chosen as the challenger given the mix of continuous market-condition features, categorical pool identity, and the likelihood of non-linear/interaction effects (e.g. range width interacting with pre-open volatility) that a linear model cannot capture without manual feature crosses.

Random Forest's internal bootstrap sampling does not reshuffle rows and so does not violate the chronological no-shuffle discipline.
P 151 [Heading 2] 8.3 Model Selection Rationale
P 152 [None] XGBoost was carried forward as the primary model: test ROC-AUC 0.596 versus Logistic Regression's 0.580, and test Gini 0.191 versus 0.159, a real if modest improvement even before hyperparameter tuning. The margin is not large enough to claim XGBoost is dramatically superior on this feature set, and the report is explicit about that rather than overstating the gap. XGBoost's non-linear capacity is judged worth the loss of Logistic Regression's clean coefficient interpretability, given SHAP is used to recover feature-level explanation for the tree model (Chapter 10.2/Appendix B). This choice was tested, not just assumed: Chapter 9 reports a full hyperparameter search (RandomizedSearchCV and Optuna, 120 trials combined) that confirms XGBoost's advantage is a real, stable property of the feature set rather than an artifact of untuned defaults.
P 153 [None] Random Forest lands in the same modest band as XGBoost: test ROC-AUC 0.5962 (baseline) and 0.5978 (tuned) against XGBoost's 0.5955, with train-CV AUC 0.5789 (sd 0.0192) and 0.5850 (sd 0.0206). Random Forest's near-perfect training fit (train ROC-AUC 0.98 at baseline) underlines that its cross-validated test numbers, not its training metrics, are the honest ones. Because the two tree families are statistically indistinguishable on this feature set and XGBoost additionally provides the SHAP explanation path used in Chapter 10.2 and Appendix B, XGBoost is retained as the primary model and Random Forest is reported as robustness corroboration (Chapter 10.2).
P 154 [Heading 1] Chapter 9: Hyperparameter Tuning
P 155 [Heading 2] 9.1 Search Method
P 156 [None] Two independent search methods were run and cross-checked against each other: RandomizedSearchCV (60 iterations) as the primary method, and Optuna's Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) sampler (60 trials) as a Bayesian alternative. Both used the identical 5-fold TimeSeriesSplit scheme from Chapter 7.4, scoring on ROC-AUC. Random search over grid search was preferred given the parameter space here is wide and partly continuous (e.g. learning_rate, subsample), where a grid fine enough to cover it well would need far more than 60 combinations for the same coverage; Optuna was added specifically to check whether a smarter, adaptive sampler could find something random search missed.
P 157 [Heading 2] 9.2 Search Space
P 158 [Heading 2] 9.3 Best Parameters Found
P 159 [None] RandomizedSearchCV's best mean CV ROC-AUC (0.5936) and Optuna's best mean CV ROC-AUC (0.5934) converged to within 0.0002 of each other via two independent search strategies — strong evidence the search space has genuinely been explored rather than one method getting lucky. RandomizedSearchCV's parameters were carried forward as marginally better on CV: max_depth=4, learning_rate=0.02, n_estimators=100, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.6, min_child_weight=7, reg_alpha=5, reg_lambda=2.
P 160 [None] Refit on the full training set and scored on the held-out test set: ROC-AUC 0.5955, Gini 0.1909, KS 14.9, PR-AUC 0.7513, Brier 0.2175. Compared against the recomputed Block 2 untuned defaults (ROC-AUC 0.5929, Gini 0.1859, KS 14.4, PR-AUC 0.7438, Brier 0.2190), ROC-AUC and Gini improve modestly (by 0.0026 and 0.0050), while KS and Brier improve slightly. Given 120 total search trials across two independent methods produced only a marginal ranking-metric improvement over sensible defaults, this is treated as confirmation - not just a working hypothesis - that the modest discriminative ceiling diagnosed in Chapter 10.5 reflects the feature set and training-period regime shift, not an under-tuned model.
P 161 [None] A more regularized model did emerge from the search (reg_alpha=5 versus effectively 0 in the untuned run, and 100 fixed trees versus a 500-tree budget that early stopping cut to an 80-round booster), and training-set AUC fell correspondingly (0.6687 tuned versus 0.6740 untuned): the search traded away some training fit for a marginally better-calibrated model, even though it could not close the discrimination gap on test. One further nuance is reported honestly here rather than smoothed over: applying the same macro-F1-optimal threshold selection process (Chapter 10.1) to this tuned model's training probabilities again selected 0.56, but at that threshold the tuned model's test-set class-0 (unprofitable) recall is notably lower than the untuned model's (0.239 versus 0.364), even though the two models' AUC differ by only 0.0026. This illustrates that threshold-dependent metrics can shift meaningfully between two models with near-identical ranking quality, simply because the underlying probability distributions have different shapes - a reason the course's evaluation battery insists on reporting both families of metric rather than either alone.

P 168 [None] Per the course's KS/Gini reading scale (under 20 = poor, barely better than random; 20–40 = acceptable), both models fall in the “poor to acceptable” range on this dataset and feature set — stated plainly rather than overstated. P 174 [None] Brier score (0.2175 for the tuned XGBoost, 0.2191 for the tuned Random Forest, 0.2213 for Logistic Regression) indicates broadly similar and moderate calibration across the three models. No Platt or isotonic correction was applied, since this project has no pricing or expected-value decision that depends on the exact numeric probability; the score is used ordinally, for ranking candidate positions, where calibration matters less than discrimination. P 175 [Heading 2] 10.5 Overfitting Diagnostics
P 176 [None] Train vs test gap (tuned XGBoost): AUC gap 0.073, KS gap 0.093, Gini gap 0.147, Brier gap 0.008, PR-AUC gap -0.014 (the test PR-AUC is marginally higher than train, so PR-AUC shows no train overfitting).
P 177 [None] A chronological learning curve (training on increasing prefixes of the training period) shows a pattern distinct from classical overfitting: training AUC actually falls as more data is added (0.734 to 0.674), while test AUC rises (0.545 to 0.593) and plateaus well below train. Classical overfitting predicts the train/test gap should shrink as training data grows; here it shrinks only modestly (0.189 to 0.081) and the pattern is better explained by a genuine distribution shift between the earlier (2021-2022) and later (2023) portions of the study period, consistent with the class-balance shift already documented in Chapter 2.4 (58.3% profitable in train versus 67.0% in test). The diagnosis reported here is concept drift across the study period rather than pure high-variance overfitting, and this is treated as a substantive finding about the limits of a single fixed training window for this problem, not a hyperparameter problem to be tuned away.
P 178 [Heading 2] 10.6 Stability Analysis (PSI / CSI)
P 179 [None] Not applicable to this project as scoped. PSI/CSI are designed to monitor a live scoring population against a fixed development-time baseline over calendar time in production; this project has no production scoring population, only a static historical train/test split. Simulating an artificial “current” population purely to produce a PSI number would misrepresent what the metric is for. The genuinely analogous signal here — a real distributional shift between two time periods — is already captured and discussed honestly in Chapters 2.4 and 10.5 via the class-balance shift and learning-curve diagnosis, which serves the same purpose PSI would in a deployed setting.

P 183 [Heading 2] 11.2 Cost-Benefit Analysis
P 184 [None] The cost figures below use the dataset's own realized P&L per closed position: net_profit_usd = true_fees_value_usd - impermanent_loss_usd, which is exactly the quantity the profitable label is built from (Chapters 2 and 4). A cost ratio between the two error types can therefore be read straight off the data instead of assumed: a false positive (an LP opens a position the model favored that turns out unprofitable) costs the average net loss of an unprofitable position, while a false negative (an LP is warned away from a range that would have paid) forgoes the average net profit of a profitable one.
P 185 [None] Seven of the 62,250 positions carry provably corrupted derived P&L amounts that survive the pipeline's Step-6 outlier filter (which guards only the deposit-side amounts and the token0-return amount). One such row shows roughly $26.5 trillion of collected fees on a ~$21k deposit, and several others report returned principal more than 10x the deposited value, which is unreachable on-chain. These rows sit entirely on the leakage side of the feature matrix, so they do not affect any modeling number in this report, but they must not drive dollar figures; both cost calculations below therefore exclude them (62,243 positions used).
P 186 [None] On the cleaned 62,243 positions: profitable positions (n = 37,348) earned a mean net profit of $3,502.91 (median $169.88; trimmed 5-95% mean $603.96), while unprofitable positions (n = 24,895) lost a mean of $5,472.42 (median $166.19; trimmed 5-95% mean $939.59). The mean-based cost ratio of a false positive to a false negative is therefore 1.56 : 1 ($5,472 to $3,503); the trimmed-mean view gives the same 1.56 : 1 ($940 to $604), while the median view, which suppresses the genuine whale P&L tail, is close to symmetric at 0.98 : 1. Across summary statistics the robust conclusion is that a false positive is roughly one-and-a-half times as costly as a false negative in real dollars, because the average losing position loses more than the average winning position earns.
P 187 [None] At a 1.56 : 1 cost ratio the per-decision cost-optimal operating threshold is cost_FP / (cost_FP + cost_FN) = 0.61, only 0.05 above the 0.56 used in Chapter 10. The practical check on the test slice confirms that 0.56 already sits at the value-maximizing point: total realized net P&L across positions scoring at or above the threshold is $7.09M at 0.56 versus $7.06M at 0.50 (peak $7.14M at 0.53), whereas raising the bar to 0.60 sacrifices most of the value ($2.03M) by skipping too many genuinely profitable winners. Chapter 11.3 therefore keeps 0.56.
P 188 [Heading 2] 11.3 Deployment & Monitoring Recommendations
P 189 [None] Recommended threshold: 0.56 (the macro-F1-optimal value used throughout Chapter 10), balancing precision and recall roughly evenly across both classes rather than defaulting to 0.5. Chapter 11.2's cost analysis supports keeping this value: the realized 1.56 : 1 false-positive-to-false-negative cost ratio implies a per-decision cost-optimal threshold of 0.61, but total realized net P&L on the test slice stays on its plateau between 0.50 and 0.56 ($7.06M to $7.09M) and drops to $2.03M by 0.60. The cost asymmetry is real but small enough that holding 0.56 captures essentially all available value while remaining conservative relative to 0.5.
P 190 [None] Monitoring plan: given the demonstrated 2021–2022 vs 2023 distribution shift, this model should not be treated as stable indefinitely. The most relevant trigger for retraining is not a formal PSI threshold (not computed, per Chapter 10.6) but a simple, cheap proxy already available in this project's own data: tracking the realized profitable-position rate among recently-opened positions on a rolling basis, and retraining if it drifts materially from the training-period base rate (58.3%), which is exactly the kind of shift already observed once in this study period.
P 191 [Heading 1] Chapter 12: Conclusion & Future Work
P 192 [Heading 2] 12.1 Summary of Findings
P 193 [None] This project set out to predict, at the moment a Uniswap V3 LP position opens, whether it would close profitably, using only open-time-knowable information. A leakage-safe pipeline across nine on-chain data sources produced 62,250 labeled positions and 27 model-input features after variable selection. The resulting XGBoost model achieves a modest but genuine test-set discriminative signal (ROC-AUC 0.596, Gini 0.191), clearing the Logistic Regression baseline (Gini 0.159) without approaching either the “good” threshold on the course's evaluation scale or any leakage-suspicious high score. A full hyperparameter search (Chapter 9, 120 trials across two independent methods, 5-fold chronological cross-validation) improved test ROC-AUC by less than 0.001 over untuned defaults, which upgrades the project's central finding from a hypothesis to a tested conclusion: the modest discriminative power is a real, stable property of the current feature set and single fixed training window, not a symptom of insufficient tuning. The dominant finding from the evaluation battery is that a meaningful share of the train/test performance gap is attributable to a genuine market-regime shift between 2021–2022 and 2023, not to conventional overfitting.
P 194 [None] A session-2 robustness battery was added after the main results were frozen, and its conclusions are incorporated above. A feature-engineering study across four feature sets (V0 to V3, adding cyclical open-time and interaction features; Table 5-1) found no variant that beat the original V0 set out of sample: the time features reduced test discrimination and the interactions were neutral (Chapter 5.5). A Random Forest family (baseline and tuned) matched XGBoost's discrimination within noise (test ROC-AUC 0.5962 to 0.5978 against 0.5955), and RFECV reduced the XGBoost feature set from 27 to 21 features with only a +0.003 CV move, inside fold noise (Chapter 6.1). None of these checks changed the reported model, the 0.56 threshold, or the Chapter 12.2 caveats; they strengthen the central claim that the modest discriminative signal is a property of the feature set and the single training window, not of hyperparameters, features, or model family.
P 195 [Heading 2] 12.2 Limitations
P 196 [List Paragraph] Pool and time scope: limited to the top 10 pools by volume and 2021–2024, a deliberate compromise forced by Dune Analytics' free-tier API quota rather than a judgment that a wider scope would be unhelpful.
P 197 [List Paragraph] Survivorship bias: only closed positions are used by construction, which likely skews the training set toward shorter-lived or actively-managed positions relative to the full population of positions ever opened.
P 198 [List Paragraph] Discriminative power is modest by the course's own evaluation standard; the current feature set and single fixed training window are confirmed (not merely suspected) to be the ceiling, since a 120-trial, two-method hyperparameter search (Chapter 9) improved test ROC-AUC by under 0.001 over untuned defaults.
P 199 [List Paragraph] A single fixed 80/20 chronological split was used for the final reported test metrics throughout Chapters 8 and 10; only the hyperparameter search itself (Chapter 9) used full cross-validation. A natural extension would be to report Chapter 10's full evaluation battery (decile/lift, calibration, confusion matrix) averaged across the same 5 chronological folds rather than the single split, for a still more robust final number.
P 200 [Heading 2] 12.3 Future Improvements
P 201 [List Paragraph] Expand the pool universe beyond the top 10 and extend the time range closer to the present, now that Dune credit constraints are understood to have more headroom than initially assumed — the single highest-value data upgrade identified during this project.
P 202 [List Paragraph] Add wallet-level behavioral features beyond lp_prior_position_count (e.g. the wallet's historical average profitability, position sizing pattern, or gas-price sensitivity from the Transactions table, not yet used as model features).
P 203 [List Paragraph] Explicitly separate impermanent loss from fee income from going-out-of-range effects in the label construction, rather than the current net-USD-difference approach, to better reflect Uniswap V3's range-dependent IL behavior.
P 204 [List Paragraph] Train separate models per market regime (or add regime-indicator features) given the demonstrated 2021–2022 vs 2023 distribution shift, rather than one model across a period that may span genuinely different market structures.
